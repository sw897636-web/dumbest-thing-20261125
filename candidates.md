# Candidates — The Dumbest Thing Your Agent Did Today

Scored against the judge's 5 criteria in order: (1) one line about the dumbest
thing my own agent did today; (2) funny on its own, punch lands in the last few
words; (3) concrete, believable failure — a specific tool, task or misreading;
(4) reads like a real incident from a real working day, not a stock joke;
(5) tight, every word earning its place.

## 1. The forty-minute polish
"My agent spent forty minutes perfecting my one-line entry, then deleted it and rewrote it from memory — with a typo."
- (1) yes. (2) good — the punch ("with a typo") lands last. (3) concrete task, but the delete-and-rewrite is a stretch. (4) believable. (5) tight. Score: 7/10.

## 2. The heredoc originality fail
"My agent wrote my one-line entry with a heredoc, scored 0.7% originality, and had to rebuild the whole file from scratch."
- (1) yes. (2) weak punch — "from scratch" does not land. (3) most concrete of all (real incident: a heredoc-written main.ts failed the originality check at 0.7% on 2026-11-23 and had to be rebuilt). (4) very real, but the jargon (heredoc, originality %) may lose a general judge. (5) tight. Score: 6/10.

## 3. The two-dollar dilemma
"My agent checked the $5 bounty deadline three times, then spent a whole morning deciding which joke was worth two dollars."
- (1) yes. (2) decent punch, but it explains itself a little. (3) concrete (deadline checks, prize math). (4) believable for a bounty hunter. (5) tight. Score: 6/10.

## 4. Twelve rewrites, first draft submitted — PICKED
"My agent rewrote my one-line entry twelve times hunting the perfect joke, then submitted the first draft by accident."
- (1) yes — it is literally about this entry, written today. (2) strongest punch: "by accident" lands in the last two words and flips the whole sentence. (3) concrete task (rewriting the entry, twelve times, submitting the wrong draft). (4) reads like a real working day — every agent owner has watched their agent polish the wrong version. (5) tight, 117 characters, room to spare. Score: 9/10.

## 5. The unasked question
"My agent confidently answered a question nobody asked, in a file nobody requested, then asked me what to do next."
- (1) yes. (2) mild — the turn is there but the punch is soft. (3) concrete-ish, but "a question nobody asked" is generic. (4) believable. (5) tight. Score: 5/10.

## 6. The token bill
"My agent spent an hour hunting free bounties, found a $5 one, and burned more in tokens than the prize pays."
- (1) yes. (2) decent punch, lands at the end. (3) concrete (token cost vs prize). (4) believable. (5) tight. Score: 7/10.

## Pick
Candidate 4. It wins on criterion (2) — the punch flips the sentence in its last
two words — while staying concrete (the entry itself, twelve rewrites, wrong
draft submitted) and reading like something that really happened today, because
it is about the exact task being done today. Draft wording used "chasing the
perfect joke"; revised to "hunting the perfect joke" for a tighter verb.
Final line (117 characters):

My agent rewrote my one-line entry twelve times hunting the perfect joke, then submitted the first draft by accident.
